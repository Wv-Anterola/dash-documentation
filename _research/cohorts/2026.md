## What this year was about

Two things changed at once, and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is that the agent stopped being a feature and became a platform.
The tool registry that landed in 2025 turned out to be the thing everyone wanted
to build on: the year's work adds tools rather than adding panels. The agent can
now enumerate what is on your canvas, drive the interface, walk you through a
tutorial, check a policy against linked testimony, and plan a video scene by
scene. Nobody had to design a new integration point for any of those.

The second is that we started taking seriously what it means to give a language
model write access to somebody's documents. Dash's agent edits a persistent,
shared workspace through tool calls, and it reads content it did not write.
That is the exact configuration indirect prompt injection exploits. The
oversight study on this page exists because we could not find anyone who had
measured whether a person actually catches an injected agent's edit after it
lands, which is the fallback nearly every real deployment still relies on.

Separately, and more quietly, Dash grew a desktop build that bundles Ollama.
That matters for the same reason: the moment a workspace tool can run its models
locally, the question of what leaves your machine has a different answer.

## What we would tell next year's cohort

Merging is the hard part, not building. Of the projects above, the ones that
reached `master` were generally the ones that extended an existing registry, a
document type, a collection view, or an agent tool, rather than standing up a
parallel system next to it. The unmerged branches are not worse work; several
are more ambitious. They are just further from anything Dash already knew how
to hold.
