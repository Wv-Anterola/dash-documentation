---
title: Pinning documents to trails
description: The three ways to pin a document to a presentation trail, and what pinning with view records.
---

Pinning is the term Dash uses for adding a document, or a particular view of a
document, to a presentation trail. There are three ways to do it, and the third
behaves differently enough to be worth reading about before you use it.

For what a trail is and why it is not a slideshow, see
[trails](/concepts/trails/).

## Pin with the pin button

1. Select the document you want to add.
2. Click the pin icon on the document decoration menu.
3. The document is added to the currently active trail.

Clicking the slide in the trail pans and zooms to the relevant part.

![Screen recording: selecting a document and using its pin control to add it to the active presentation trail.](/assets/gifs/gettingstarted/dash-pin-doc.gif)

## Pin by dragging

Drag the document into empty space on the trail. Either the title bar or the
body of the document works as a drag handle.

![The Trails panel showing the destination where a dragged document becomes a new presentation step.](/assets/images/current/dash-trails-panel.webp)

## Pin with view

Hold `Shift` and click the pin icon. A slide pinned this way shows a `V` badge.

Pinning with view records the document's current view properties alongside the
reference, rather than only the document:

- `scroll`, the scroll position within the document
- `x` and `y`, the position on the 2D canvas

The `Update View` button on a slide re-captures whatever the document's current
view properties are. Pinning the same document twice with different view
properties and stepping between the slides is how you animate movement through a
document.

## What the three methods store

| Method | Adds the document | Captures contextual view state | Best use |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| Pin button | Yes | No | Return to the live document with its current state |
| Drag into trail | Yes | No | Build or reorder a trail spatially from the panel |
| Shift + pin | Yes | Yes | Restore a deliberate scroll, pan, zoom, position, or size |

The slide keeps a reference to the live document rather than a detached copy.
Changing the underlying content therefore changes what the trail presents;
captured view state controls how that content is framed.

## Verify the result

1. Open the new slide and confirm it navigates to the intended document.
2. For a view pin, change the document's framing, reopen the slide, and verify
   that the captured framing returns.
3. Use **Update View** only when you intend to replace the saved framing.
4. Reload the workspace and repeat the navigation before relying on the trail
   for a presentation.
