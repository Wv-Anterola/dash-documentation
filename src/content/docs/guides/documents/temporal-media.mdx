---
title: Temporal media
description: How Dash treats audio and video, and why a link can point at a moment in a recording rather than at the file.
---

Dash treats audio and video as documents with addressable time. You can place
them in collections, give them fields, link them to other material, and target
a moment or interval instead of only the whole recording.

Use the type-specific guides for controls:

- [Audio](/guides/documents/audio/) covers recording, playback, trimming, and
  dictation.
- [Video](/guides/documents/video/) covers imported video, playback, and the
  timeline.

## Why they get their own section

Everything else in Dash is addressed by position: a region of an image, a
passage of text, a page of a PDF. Time-based media adds another axis, and Dash
treats it as a first-class one.

Both types carry a timeline. A temporal anchor stores its position against the
media document, and a link can use that anchor as an endpoint. Following the
link can therefore seek to the relevant point or play a bounded interval
instead of opening the file at zero.

This falls directly out of the link model: an anchor is a selection rather than
a document, so a temporal selection needs no special case.
[Links and anchors](/concepts/links/) explains the model and where it is still
thin.

## A source-review workflow

1. Put the recording and the document you are writing into one collection.
2. Play or scrub to the relevant passage.
3. Create an anchor at that time or over the interval.
4. Link the anchor to a note, claim, or annotation.
5. Give the link a relationship or description so another reader knows why it
   matters.
6. Follow the link once to confirm that it opens the intended passage.

This is more durable than typing “around 12:40” into a note because the time is
part of the link endpoint. It still depends on the underlying media remaining
available.

## Shared behavior

Audio and video both use a media state, current timecode, timeline annotations,
and clip boundaries. Their document views register and unregister with Dash's
currently-playing set so playback can be coordinated with the surrounding
workspace. Timecode changes also drive timeline scrolling and can highlight
linked material as playback crosses a linked point.

Shrinking an audio document below a small size collapses it to a mini-player.
Full controls return when it has enough space.

## What differs

Audio documents can be recorded inside Dash and trimmed. Video documents are
imported and cannot be trimmed, though they carry the same timeline for
placing labels and region markers.

Audio also supports dictation, which transcribes speech to text.

## Reliability and access

- A temporal anchor does not package the media. If the referenced URL, upload,
  or local asset is unavailable, the link record can survive while playback
  fails.
- Browser media support depends on the encoded format and response headers.
- Recording requires microphone permission.
- Transcription requires its configured service and credentials.
- Sharing a Dash document does not override access controls on an external
  media host.
- Check seek behavior after replacing a media source: the old duration and
  anchors may no longer line up.

## Implementation map

`AudioBox.tsx` owns recording and audio playback. `VideoBox.tsx` owns video
playback. Both use the stacked timeline and annotatable view infrastructure;
temporal anchors are ordinary Dash documents associated with the media
document. The [links architecture](/architecture/links-trails/) follows the
anchor-to-link path in more detail.
